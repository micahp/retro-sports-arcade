From pixels to polygons

A side-by-side visual reference for hockey, basketball and football. Compare real-era screenshots and what changed between 1990s sprite sports and early-2000s 3D. These are third-party references, not Retro Sports Arcade assets.

🏒 Hockey

Overhead sprite clarity → early 3D skating, arenas and camera depth.

NHL 95

SNES gameplay · 1994 release

Small directional sprites, overhead readability, a large visible ice surface. Good reference for our first playable hockey target.

Screenshot source

NHL 2001

PlayStation 1 gameplay · 2000 release

Polygonal rink and athletes, camera depth and broadcast-style overlays. This particular frame has a large intermission graphic, so inspect more gameplay footage for movement comparison.

Screenshot source
Arcade implication: Prioritize NHL 95's readability and game feel first. Later, test low-poly models against sprite-stacked skating and shot/recovery timing.

🏀 Basketball

Angled 2D courts and animation sprites → expressive 3D player bodies.

NBA Live 95

Genesis gameplay video thumbnail · 1994 release

Isometric court, compact sprite players and recognizable passing, shooting and dunk sequences. The still is a video thumbnail, not an independently archived screenshot.

Watch gameplay source

NBA Live 2001

PlayStation 2 gameplay · 2000 release

3D court and players, more detailed shooting poses and spatial camera depth. Compare the ball-to-hand contact with The U's animation problems.

Screenshot source
Arcade implication: Test The U's pre-rendered sprites and Gridiron's sprite stacking before committing to a complex rig. Judge individual actions, not entire animation libraries.

🏈 Football

Readable sprite formations → detailed 3D blocking, passing and tackling.

Madden NFL 96

Genesis gameplay · 1995 release

A 1995-era sprite reference for player scale, field readability and movement. This is Madden NFL 96, not Madden NFL 95, and is labeled accordingly.

Screenshot source

Madden NFL 2001

PlayStation 2 gameplay video thumbnail · 2000 release

Low-poly 3D player bodies, richer stadium presentation and more complex catch/tackle animations. The thumbnail links to captured PS2 gameplay.

Watch gameplay source
Arcade implication: Start from Legendary Picks' Pixel Red Zone and Gridiron's 2D/sprite stack. Require movement and tackle validation before treating 3D complexity as an improvement.
